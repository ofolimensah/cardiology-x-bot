# PARADIGM-HF — Trial Breakdown Templates

Reference example for the trial-breakdown format. Use as a style guide for the summarizer.

## Trial Facts

- **Trial:** PARADIGM-HF (Prospective Comparison of ARNI with ACEI to Determine Impact on Global Mortality and Morbidity in Heart Failure)
- **Comparison:** Sacubitril-valsartan (LCZ696) vs enalapril
- **Population:** 8,442 patients with HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%), NYHA II–IV
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, active-controlled; run-in phase to confirm tolerability of both drugs before randomization
- **Primary endpoint:** Composite of cardiovascular death or first heart-failure hospitalization
- **Duration:** Median 27 months
- **Sponsor:** Novartis

## One-Liner Template

> PARADIGM-HF swapped enalapril for sacubitril-valsartan in 8,442 HFrEF patients and cut cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalization by 20%. Number needed to treat: 21 over 27 months. It didn't just beat the old standard — it ended thirty years of ACE-inhibitor dogma in one trial.

## Thread Template

1. PARADIGM-HF swapped enalapril for sacubitril-valsartan in 8,442 HFrEF patients and cut CV death or HF hospitalization by 20%. NNT 21 over 27 months. (link)
2. Design: randomized, double-blind, active-controlled. Every patient went through a run-in phase on both drugs first — only those who tolerated both were randomized. That's how they isolated the drug effect from tolerability noise.
3. Primary endpoint: composite of cardiovascular death or first HF hospitalization. 20% relative risk reduction. Driven by fewer deaths *and* fewer hospitalizations — not one at the expense of the other.
4. The question everyone asked: does adding neprilysin blockade raise hyperkalemia or angioedema vs ACE inhibitor alone? Answer: comparable rates. The safety profile held.
5. Bottom line: PARADIGM-HF is the trial that moved HFrEF first-line therapy from ACE inhibitors to ARNI. If you're still defaulting to enalapril in a new HFrEF patient, this is the paper to reread.

## Notes for the Bot

- Lead with the counterintuitive or practice-changing finding, not "New study."
- Always include NNT and follow-up duration when available — clinicians weight these heavily.
- Flag the run-in/enrichment design explicitly; it's the most common criticism and the most interesting design detail.
- For threads, put the safety question (hyperkalemia, angioedema, hypotension) in its own post — it's the highest-bookmark line.
