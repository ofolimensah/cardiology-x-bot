## Post 1a — Series Intro — Mon Nov 2, 6:00 AM ET
One-liner. Sets up the month-long arc.

> Over the next month, I'll walk through the trials that built modern heart failure care — from digitalis in 1785 to the four-pillar stack we use today. Each post covers one era: the trial, why it mattered, and how it changed what we prescribe. No jargon without translation. If you treat heart failure patients, this is the story of why your guidelines look the way they do.

## Post 1b — The Symptomatic Era (1785) — Mon Nov 2, 6:30 AM ET
One-liner. Digitalis: Withering's foxglove. Purely symptomatic relief, zero mortality data, 200+ years of use before anyone asked the right question.

> 1785. William Withering, a physician in Birmingham, publishes "An Account of the Foxglove." A patient with dropsy had been cured by a herbal remedy from a folk healer — Withering isolated the active plant, digitalis, and documented its effects across 160+ cases. Symptomatic relief only. No control group, no mortality endpoint. It would take two centuries before a randomized trial asked the question that mattered.

Hook: "The first heart failure drug was discovered by a country doctor watching a woman cure dropsy with herbal tea."

## Post 2 — V-HeFT (1986) — Wed Nov 4, 8 PM ET
One-liner. Vasodilators (hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate) cut mortality ~38% in HFrEF. Proof that heart failure is a treatable disease, not just a fluid problem.

Hook: "This is the trial that made cardiologists ask: if we can unload the heart, can we change its trajectory?"

## Post 3 — SOLVD (1991) — Fri Nov 6, 6 AM ET [THREAD]
Thread. Enalapril vs placebo. 20% mortality reduction, first ACE inhibitor mortality trial. Establishes ACE inhibitors as first-line disease-modifying therapy. Design detail worth a post: the prevention arm (asymptomatic LV dysfunction) showed benefit too — HF starts before symptoms.

Thread beats: (1) headline + NNT, (2) run-in design, (3) prevention arm finding, (4) why ACEI became dogma for 30 years, (5) forward hook to beta-blockers.

## Post 4 — The Beta-Blocker Paradox (setup) — Mon Nov 9, 8 PM ET
One-liner. Everything about beta-blockers said they would kill a failing heart: negative inotropy, bradycardia, they'd worsen congestion. Yet...

Hook: "In 1999, two trials broke cardiology's most confident belief."

## Post 5 — MERIT-HF + CIBIS-II (1999) — Wed Nov 11, 6 AM ET
One-liner. Metoprolol (MERIT-HF) and bisoprolol (CIBIS-II) each cut mortality ~34%. The negative inotrope saved lives. Largest relative risk reduction in HF history at the time.

Hook: "The drug that slows the heart turned out to be the one that extends it."

## Post 6 — The Beta-Blocker Flip — Fri Nov 13, 8 PM ET [THREAD — MIDPOINT CLIFFHANGER]
Thread. Full story of the dogma flip: why it was counterintuitive, the trial designs (carvedilol's COPERNICUS for severe HF), the mechanism (reverse remodeling, not just rate control), and the clinical implication — beta-blockers became mandatory, not optional. End with: "If the most dangerous-sounding drug was the most protective, what else are we wrong about?"

This is the engagement peak. Highest bookmark potential in the series.

## Post 7 — RALES (1999) — Mon Nov 16, 6 AM ET
One-liner. Spironolactone in severe HFrEF (NYHA III–IV): 30% mortality reduction. Aldosterone escape — ACE inhibitors alone don't block the RAAS fully. MRA enters GDMT.

Hook: "A 40-year-old diuretic, nearly forgotten, resurfaces as a mortality drug."

## Post 8 — EMPHASIS-HF (2011) — Wed Nov 18, 8 PM ET
One-liner. Eplerenone extends MRA benefit to mild HFrEF (NYHA II). The class graduates from "rescue drug for dying patients" to "foundational therapy for everyone."

Hook: "Heart failure treatment stopped being about the sickest patients and started being about all of them."

## Post 9 — PARADIGM-HF (2014) — Fri Nov 20, 6 AM ET
One-liner or short thread. Sacubitril-valsartan vs enalapril: 20% RRR on CV death/HF hospitalization. ARNI replaces ACE inhibitor as first-line. Reference: templates/paradigm_hf.md.

Hook: "Thirty years of ACE-inhibitor dogma ended in one trial."

## Post 10 — DAPA-HF + EMPEROR-Reduced (2019–2020) — Mon Nov 23, 8 PM ET
One-liner. SGLT2 inhibitors: dapagliflozin and empagliflozin cut HF events ~25% in HFrEF, on top of full background therapy. First new pillar in a decade.

Hook: "A diabetes drug walked into a heart failure trial and rewrote the guidelines."

## Post 11 — EMPEROR-Preserved + DELIVER (2021–2022) — Wed Nov 25, 6 AM ET
One-liner. SGLT2i benefit extends to HFpEF — the first therapy proven across the entire ejection fraction spectrum. The "no treatment for preserved EF" era ends.

Hook: "For 40 years, half of all heart failure had no proven therapy. One drug class closed that gap."

## Post 12 — Current GDMT — Fri Nov 27, 8 PM ET [THREAD — FINALE]
Thread. The four-to-five pillar stack: ARNI, evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i, plus diuretics for congestion. The arc recap: each generation stacked, nobody replaced anybody. Close with the open question — what's the next pillar? Amyloidosis therapies (tafamidis), devices, the pipeline.

Thread beats: (1) the stack visualized, (2) the stacking principle as the series thesis, (3) what each pillar interrupts, (4) the remaining gaps (HFpEF still undertreated, residual risk), (5) forward-looking close.
